# Datasheet: AI-HDL 2026 Dataset

This datasheet follows Gebru et al., "Datasheets for Datasets" (CACM 2021).

## Motivation

**Why was the dataset created?**
Public datasets for LLM-based hardware design mostly contain single-turn RTL generation problems scored by simulation. Physical-design datasets start from a netlist. No public dataset links natural-language design intent and the iterative human–LLM conversation to RTL revisions, PPA, security and sign-off results for the same designs. AI-HDL fills that gap.

## Composition

**What do the instances represent?**
One instance is a team's design trajectory. It is the team's submissions for up to four design phases (DP1 functional RTL, DP2 PPA optimization, DP3 security hardening, DP4 physical design) of a custom peripheral attached to the TinyQV RISC-V core.

**How many instances are there?**
21 teams from the 2026 cycle (20 of the 22 second-cycle finalists plus one non-finalist), and 11 teams from the 2025 pilot cycle (`pilot_2025/`; FPGA digital-watch designs without layouts). The five teams with prompt logs for all four phases and a DP4 layout, and the two designs submitted for fabrication, are marked in `index.csv`.

**What does each instance contain?**
- RTL and its revisions
- testbenches (Verilog, C++ / Verilator, cocotb)
- synthesis, timing, power and OpenLane reports
- gate-level netlists
- GDSII / DEF layouts (for teams that completed DP4)
- design documentation in Markdown
- the team's prompt logs, in their original format or converted to Markdown

**Is there a label or target?**
- **Tool outputs** from the open-source flow: simulation results, synthesis cell counts, timing slack, power, DRC / LVS results.
- **Phase gating:** the 20 released finalists passed all four phase gates; their final-leaderboard rank is in `index.csv`. Mercer is not a finalist.

None of these is an independent verification: gates were judged by graders against a rubric, power values are vectorless estimates, and functional evidence is limited to each team's own testbenches.

**Is anything missing?**
Yes:
- Two finalist teams' submissions.
- Prompt logs for several teams or phases (listed in README).
- Binary documents (design-report PDFs, Word files, slides, screenshots), which were removed during anonymization.
- Files from the shared TinyQV template.

**Are there recommended splits?**
No. This release does not define a benchmark: with five complete trajectories, model results would not be stable. For any training or evaluation use, keep splits team-disjoint (no team in more than one of train, validation and test), so that a team's design and phrasing do not leak between them.

**Does the dataset contain personal data?**
Student and mentor names, emails, GitHub / LinkedIn handles and home-directory user names were replaced with pseudonyms or placeholders. Team names, institutions and countries are retained, since they appear on the public competition leaderboard. Free text was processed with rule-based replacement, so residual personal references may remain. Please report any to the maintainers.

## Collection process

**How was the data collected?**
Teams submitted GitHub repositories or folders at the end of each design phase, from January to May 2026. Hardware had to be generated by prompting an LLM. Prompt logs were graded, and manual edits had to be declared, though declarations may be incomplete. The 2025 pilot cycle ran from October 2024 to March 2025.

**Who was involved, and were they compensated?**
Across both cycles, 776 participants registered in 148 teams; the 2026 cycle had 604 participants in 108 teams, from 30 universities and 6 community colleges in 19 countries. These self-reported registration figures describe the competition, not the released subset of 32 teams. Participation was voluntary; prizes were awarded to winners.

## Preprocessing

- **De-duplication:** files are de-duplicated across cumulative phase snapshots.
- **Template removal:** files shared by three or more teams (the template) are left out.
- **Prompt logs:** PDF and Word logs are converted to text.
- **Anonymization:** rule-based (`scripts/anon_rules.py`).
- **Unaltered:** prompt text and RTL content are otherwise unchanged.

## Uses

**Intended uses:**
- studying multi-turn, tool-feedback-driven human–LLM RTL design across stages
- studying how designers new to hardware describe hardware intent
- building candidate tasks for future benchmarks: tool-log-guided RTL repair, constraint-preserving PPA or security revision, and early prediction of post-layout metrics from RTL

**Uses to avoid:**
- ranking or assessing individual students or institutions
- treating the designs as production IP or as security-certified implementations (no side-channel or attack evaluation was performed)
- treating power figures as measurements
- drawing causal conclusions about the effect of LLMs on hardware design (the competition had no comparison group)
